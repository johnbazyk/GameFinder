# Historical record: original 0.2 stress test

# GameFinder: 25-game rubric stress test
**Assessor:** ChatGPT • **Date:** 14 November 2026 • **Standard:** current calibration edition 0.2 • **Status:** proposed desk assessments; no publication approval

## Decision in plain language
**Keep the architecture, tighten the boundaries, and do not bulk-publish these scores yet.** The rubric produces useful distinctions between chance, planning, rules burden, motor execution, communication and spatial decisions. It also exposes ambiguous boundaries that can change which games a user receives. The greatest risks are the Party threshold, Board/Spatial meaning, Skill versus Strategy, and an overbroad Resource Management definition. The 0.5 increments are usable recording categories but not yet demonstrated levels of reproducible precision.

This test produced **25 game profiles, 250 provisional numeric judgments and 25 unassessed Family fields**. Luck and Strategy are shared between dimensions and vibes, so there are 11 distinct constructs rather than 13 separately scored constructs. Five first-pass values were revised in self-audit; three revisions crossed a vibe qualification threshold. These counts describe this assessment process, not rubric accuracy or independent reviewer agreement. No games were played or simulated, no people were observed, and no production code was executed.

## What was tested—and what was not
The set follows the governing document’s 25–30-game coverage brief: deterministic planning, chance, motor performance, visual recognition, word construction, resource engines, spatial tiles, negotiation, cooperative puzzles and party communication. It is a purposive stress sample, not a representative catalog or statistically powered validation sample. One explicit edition/mode/player scope per title was assessed. Alternate modes and player counts were identified as risks, not independently tested.

Method: read publisher-authored rules or official descriptions; record an initial score and rationale against the current anchors; perform a same-assessor adversarial check; preserve changed first-pass values; run a small independent reference implementation of eligibility semantics and threshold sensitivity. Most evidence establishes mechanisms. Claims about centrality, engagement, repeated play and the exact half-point placement remain interpretations. Publisher marketing classifications were not treated as proof of numeric fit.

All numeric cells below are **proposed**, even when the underlying rule is explicit. All Family cells are **unassessed**: there is no recorded youngest child age, novice participation, adult assistance or observed agency for these sessions. Party and Replayability numbers are design-based hypotheses, not measured group reactions or repeat-play outcomes. No reviewer identity or review date is fabricated. The five self-corrections do not fulfill the two-reviewer calibration requirement.

## Per-game evidence and assessment notes
Each URL identifies the source used, not an endorsement of our score. Accessed 14 November 2026. Primary-source rules support the mechanics; the numeric judgments are GameFinder interpretations. Some links redirect or intermittently fail, so source preservation is required before publication.

### 1. Chess
**Scope:** FIDE 2023 basic rules; 2 players; untimed; no competition administration.  **Status:** proposed; Family unassessed.

**Evidence:** Alternating legal moves on a fixed grid; checkmate objective, captures, promotion and special moves. [Source](https://handbook.fide.com/chapter/e012023).

**Rationale:** No chance mechanism; sustained planning and spatial decisions dominate. Pattern recognition motivates Skill 3, but may duplicate Strategy. Rules exceptions motivate Complexity 2.5 rather than simple-procedure 2 or multiple-systems 3. Material sacrifices motivate Resources 3 under the broad asset definition. No required negotiation or motor challenge; renewable positions suggest Replayability 5.

**Before approval:** Skill/Strategy overlap; pieces as resources; low Social does not mean players never interact. Replayability is a structural hypothesis, not observed retention.

### 2. Connect Four
**Scope:** Hasbro classic; 2 players; original drop-only mode.  **Status:** proposed; Family unassessed.

**Evidence:** Players alternate dropping checkers and attempt a four-in-a-row while preventing the opponent doing so. [Source](https://www.hasbro.com/common/instruct/ConnectFour.PDF).

**Initial rationale (see correction table for revisions):** Deterministic spatial planning with a very small rule burden. Recognition supports Skill 2; planning supports Strategy 4. Columns constrain future placements, tentatively Resources 2. Handling is administrative. Opponent obstruction does not satisfy our communication-based Social scale. Replayability 3 reflects changing decisions, with no claim of inexhaustible depth.

**Before approval:** Whether ordinary positional planning should count again as Skill or Resources.

### 3. Candy Land
**Scope:** Hasbro A4813 spinner edition; 3 players; standard rules, no younger-player variant.  **Status:** proposed; Family unassessed.

**Evidence:** Spinner determines movement; path includes occupied-space handling, shortcuts and missed turns. [Source](https://instructions.hasbro.com/api/download/A4813_en-us_Candy-Land-Game.pdf).

**Initial rationale (see correction table for revisions):** Chance determines progress without outcome-changing choices. Complexity 1.5 allows simple exceptions beyond one procedure. Replayability 1 follows the rubric's meaningful-challenge definition, not children's enjoyment. Party 2 is only a shared-event hypothesis. Initial Board 3 treats consequential path movement as sufficient.

**Before approval:** Board wording says meaningful decisions, which this game lacks; Family may under-credit shared play without decision agency.

### 4. Jenga
**Scope:** Hasbro 2000 rules; 3 players; competitive one-hand play.  **Status:** proposed; Family unassessed.

**Evidence:** Remove a block below the highest completed level, then place it atop the tower; collapse determines loss. [Source](https://www.hasbro.com/common/instruct/jenga.pdf).

**Rationale:** Physical precision is decisive; block choice adds immediate tactics. Luck 1 excludes uncontrolled motor error from random mechanisms. Simple restrictions justify Complexity 1.5. Renewed physical configurations suggest Replayability 3. Watching each removal suggests Party 3.5, not observed enjoyment. Board 4 follows consequential physical position and support.

**Before approval:** Board definition may exclude literal structures as non-representational; no physical practice occurred; physical variability versus luck is unresolved.

### 5. Twister
**Scope:** Hasbro 98831; 3 active players plus caller; spinner mode including Air/Choice.  **Status:** proposed; Family unassessed.

**Evidence:** Random calls require limb placement; falling or knee/elbow contact eliminates a player. This edition adds Air and Spinner's Choice. [Source](https://instructions.hasbro.com/en-us/instruction/twister).

**Initial rationale (see correction table for revisions):** Random requirements and physical execution coexist. Positioning offers immediate tactics and limited space choices. Simple exceptions suggest Complexity 1.5. Spatial reach makes Board 4 plausible and shared performance suggests Party 4. Replayability 3 is provisional renewed physical challenge.

**Before approval:** Publisher overview retrieved; PDF could not be parsed. Balance/flexibility are not fine-motor dexterity; mat position fits literal Board but may miss user intent. Physical access and body-size differences need observation.

### 6. UNO
**Scope:** Mattel W2085 108-card rules; 4 players; match to 500; no stacking house rules.  **Status:** proposed; Family unassessed.

**Evidence:** Match color/number/symbol, draw when needed, and use action cards; Wild Draw Four has a challenge rule. [Source](https://service.mattel.com/instruction_sheets/W2085.pdf).

**Rationale:** Draw order strongly affects options; sequencing and color choices add tactics below sustained Strategy 3. Remembering colors supports modest Skill. Action and challenge exceptions justify Complexity 2. Timed use of a hand supports Resources 2.5, subject to the hand-as-resource boundary. Party 3 is a shared-event hypothesis; no spatial or motor win condition.

**Before approval:** Is hand shedding resource management? Party 2.5/3 boundary. Rules extraction initially merged Reverse and Wild Draw Four; checked the corrected action section.

### 7. SET
**Scope:** PlayMonster standard 81-card game; 4 players; no easy-start variant.  **Status:** proposed; Family unassessed.

**Evidence:** Identify three cards satisfying four feature rules; call SET before collecting; the first call controls access. [Source](https://www.playmonster.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/SETRules.pdf).

**Initial rationale (see correction table for revisions):** Visual recognition dominates; common shuffled challenges justify some chance without random resolution. Little connected planning. Announcements/checking are limited Social. Simple procedures justify Complexity 1.5 despite a hard cognitive task. Initial Dexterity 3 treats speed as physical. Card layout organizes visibility rather than positional scoring. Replayability 4 is inferred renewed recognition challenge.

**Before approval:** Call-first rule tests cognitive speed versus physical dexterity. Random task generation is not necessarily high outcome luck.

### 8. Spot It! Minions
**Scope:** Asmodee-hosted Minions rulebook; The Tower; 4 players.  **Status:** proposed; Family unassessed.

**Evidence:** Players match their top card to the central card, call the icon, and collect it; physical action breaks a calling tie. [Source](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-asmodeeca/uploads/2023/07/SpotitMinions_FR_Rules.pdf).

**Rationale:** Recognition drives success; random individual comparisons make Luck 2.5 plausible rather than clearly weak or decisive. No meaningful long-term plan. Dexterity 2 captures a limited tie-breaking role, not all speed. Announcements do not establish sustained Social. Party 3 and Replayability 3 remain experience hypotheses.

**Before approval:** Do not generalize this mode or themed icon set to every Spot It edition; Party threshold depends on engagement evidence.

## Proposed clarification text—not a silent change to the standard
1. **Spatial Play:** “Score the dependence of consequential choices on position, adjacency, distance, connectivity or physical support. Mere storage, identity lookup, layout decoration, or display of a scalar answer is insufficient. Record physical-board presence separately.” This proposed wording would include Jenga and Twister for spatial choices while excluding the Wavelength dial as answer display; the inclusion itself needs user-comprehension testing.
2. **Resource Management:** “Name the scarce pool or budget and the allocation mechanism. Explain the competing uses or opportunity cost. A normal turn, occupied square, or inventory of pieces is not sufficient by itself. The same mechanism may support more than one construct only when each has a distinct rationale.”
3. **Party:** “A qualifying rating requires recurring expressive, conversational, performative or shared-reaction activity as a central intended experience. Shared attention to solving a strategic problem, by itself, is insufficient.” This would reduce cooperative over-inclusion, but must be tested against quiet party games and varied player counts before replacing the anchor.
4. **Skill:** “Identify the execution task and the rule-level role it plays. Distinguish retrieving/recognizing information or performing a motor task from selecting and evaluating a plan. If the distinction cannot be justified, hold the score provisional.” This is a reviewer aid, not proof that cognitive skill can always be separated cleanly.
5. **Luck evidence:** “Identify whether randomness changes setup, available options or action resolution; whether exposure is shared or asymmetric; and what mitigation players have. Absence of an explicit randomizer does not by itself settle all physical-uncertainty cases.”
6. **Evidence display:** “Rules-based proposal,” “human-reviewed assessment,” and “observed mixed-age fit” are different evidence statements. None should be labeled scientifically validated by default.
